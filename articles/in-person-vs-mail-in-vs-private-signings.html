There are three ways to get an autograph at a signing: show up in person, mail in your item (or buy one), or order from a private signing. In person gets you the meeting and the photo. Mail-in gets you the signature without the trip. Private signings are often the cheapest way to get a big name, but you never see the athlete.

Every listing on Autograph Hero is tagged with its signing type, so it helps to know what each one means before you buy.

The three signing types

In-person signings

You buy an autograph ticket, wait in line and get your item signed while you watch. These happen at card shows, conventions, memorabilia stores and team events.

  • Pros: you meet the athlete, can usually get a photo, and you know the signature is real because you saw it signed.
  • Cons: you have to travel, lines can be long, and tickets for big names often sell out.
  • Tip: buy autograph tickets ahead of time. Many shows cap how many each athlete will sign, and prices often go up at the door.

Mail-in signings

You send your own item to the promoter before the signing, or buy an item they supply. The athlete signs it at the in-person event or a separate session, and the promoter ships it back to you.

  • Pros: no travel, and you can get athletes who sign far from where you live.
  • Cons: you have to meet the order deadline, shipping costs both ways, and it can take weeks to get your item back.
  • Tip: read the promoter's packing instructions carefully, and pay for tracking and insurance on valuable items.

Private signings

The athlete signs a batch of items in a closed session with no fans present. You preorder a piece, or buy one afterward from the promoter's stock.

  • Pros: often the lowest price for a big name, and the easiest way to get athletes who rarely do public signings.
  • Cons: no meeting, no photo and less choice over the item. You're relying on the promoter's word that the athlete signed it.
  • Tip: stick with established promoters, and ask for a signing photo or third-party authentication.

Choose in person if the experience matters as much as the signature, or the athlete is signing near you.

Choose mail-in if you want a specific item signed by someone who isn't coming to your area.

Choose private if you want a star's autograph at the best price and don't mind missing the meeting.

FAQ

Are mail-in and private autographs real?

From reputable promoters, yes. The athlete signs your item in person, just without you there. For extra peace of mind, add third-party authentication (PSA, JSA or Beckett) where the promoter offers it.

Can I get an inscription or personalization?

Usually, for an extra fee. Inscriptions are typically available at all three types of signing, but the options and prices are set by each promoter.

Why is the same athlete's autograph priced differently at different signings?

The promoter sets the price. It depends on the item size, the market and how much demand there is. That's why it pays to compare listings for the same athlete.

What if I miss the mail-in deadline?

Check whether the promoter is selling items from the signing afterward. Many do, often as private signing stock.
