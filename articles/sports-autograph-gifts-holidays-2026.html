A signed item from a fan's favorite player is one of the few gifts that's personal, collectible and usually goes up in value. The best part is that you don't need to find one on the secondhand market. Hundreds of athletes do signings every month, and many of them take mail-in orders, so you can have an autograph made out to the person you're buying for, sometimes with a personal inscription.

This guide covers what to buy at each budget, how to time a mail-in order so it arrives before the holidays, and how to make sure what you buy is real. Every signing mentioned here can be found on Autograph Hero, where you can filter by sport, team and signing type.

Gift ideas by fan type

  • The superfan: a signed item inscribed with a moment they remember (a championship year or a famous play).
  • The new collector: a signed card or photo plus a display case or frame.
  • The kid: a personalized photo made out to them by name. It's hard to beat seeing their own name next to their hero's signature.
  • The fan who has everything: a ticket to an in-person signing or card show, so they get the meeting as well as the autograph.

Timing: order now if it has to arrive by the holidays

Mail-in signings rarely ship the same week. The signing itself is on a set date, and then the promoter has to process, authenticate and ship every order. Allow for that when you're buying a gift.

  1. Check the signing date first. If the athlete signs after mid-December, the item probably won't arrive before the holidays.
  2. Check the order deadline. Most mail-in signings stop taking orders several days to a few weeks before the signing date.
  3. Allow for shipping back. Turnaround after the signing varies by promoter, from about a week to several weeks. Authentication adds time.
  4. Have a plan B. If it won't make it, print a photo of the player and a note saying the autograph is on the way. It still makes a great gift.

If you're short on time, look for private signings where the item is already signed and in stock, or in-person signings in your city before the holidays. Not sure how the types differ? Read In-Person vs Mail-In vs Private Signings.

Make sure it's real

The safest autograph is one you watched being signed, or one ordered straight from the signing. If you're buying an item that's already signed, follow these rules:

  • Buy from the signing's promoter whenever you can. Items from a promoter's own signing come with proof of where they were signed.
  • Look for third-party authentication on higher-priced items (PSA, JSA or Beckett). It costs more, but it makes the item easier to resell and gives the person you're buying for peace of mind.
  • Be wary of prices that seem too good. A star's signed jersey selling for a fraction of the going rate is a warning sign.
  • Keep the paperwork. Put the certificate of authenticity or receipt in the gift box.
